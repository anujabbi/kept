# EM report, 2026-11-26

Fourth pass, run because the owner reported two usability problems after #205 shipped and asked
who should have caught them:

- **A weekly goal set up from a preset can't change its unit.** #206 hides the unit chips for
  preset names (`WeeklyGoalDraft.showsUnits`). The onboarding sheet from #207 has no target
  control at all; it only shows "Exercise, 30 min, 4 days a week".
- **Home after onboarding leaves too little room for the habits and the weekly card.** Sprig and

**Totals, 29 Oct to 25 Nov:** 40 cards, 0 regressions, 16 review rounds, 8 failing QA comments on
7 cards, 0 escalations, 0 questions. The old counter said 4 questions. All 4 were false: three

## Who should have caught them

No step in the pipeline was asked to judge usability, so every agent did its job and these still
got through:

- **Reviewer:** reads code only.
- **Dev and QA:** prove the acceptance list. Every layout check we have (Daybreak's "fit and
  finish" list, the goldens, the font-scale checks) asks "is anything clipped or overlapping?",
  never "does this get enough room?".
- **Planner:** turns the owner's design into a spec without looking at a screen.

Both problems were decided before any code existed, and in my opinion each was seen and let go:

| Problem | Where it was decided | Who saw it | What happened |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unit hidden for preset weekly goals | **Planner's** #206 plan copied the habit screen's `showsUnits` rule | Dev flagged it in the PR | **Reviewer** approved it as "loose wording" in the acceptance list. The onboarding sheet's missing target control traces back to #205's own design. |
| Not enough room on Home | **#165's Home mockup** shows four habits and the weekly card on one screen, because it leaves out the real Home's "Last 7 days" and Shield. Building to it, dev on #174 wrote a `DECISIONS.md` line: the add row sits half below the fold with two habits and needs a scroll with three. | Reviewer, and QA on #174 | Both recorded it as fact. The 22 Nov EM report saw the weekly section was below the fold in every Home golden and treated it as noise. |

My first answer was QA, which is the only step that uses the running app. The owner's answer is
better: **QA is too late.** By then the screen is built, and fixing it means a new card. Both
problems were visible in the plan, if the plan had shown the real screen. So the user's-eye review
moves to the **planner**. QA keeps one job here. Of 10 usability notes QA wrote in the window, 6
never became a card (#123 chip, #128, #132, #134, #168, #207).

## Changes (PR for #215, needs the owner)

1. **`planner`, new section "Show the screens first".** For a card a user can see:
   - Screenshot today's real screens on the emulator.
   - Mock the "after" screens in HTML at 412 × 915, copying every part the card doesn't change
     at its real size.
   - Render them with headless Chrome.
   - Review them as a first-time user with three questions: is it on the first screen; can the
     user change what is shown; does the next screen make sense?
   - Refine for up to two rounds.
   - Attach the images to the plan comment.

   Anything that would change the owner's design goes to the owner as a question. **Incidents:**
   today's two problems, and the #165 mockup that hid the room problem by leaving parts out.
2. **`qa` step 6a and `dev` step 7: usability notes and visible trade-offs become cards.**
   - Dev lists under **For the owner** any control hidden, anything pushed below the fold, or
     any visible difference from the mockups.
   - QA files each of those, and every usability note, as a Backlog card with the `usability`
     label. These cards never block the card under test.

   **Incidents:** the 6 lost notes, #174, and #206.
3. **`em-stats.sh`: a `?` inside a link, code or a quote no longer counts as a question.** The
   count for this window goes from 4 to 0.

**The cost:** the planner now needs an emulator and a build for every card that changes a screen.
The `usability` label was created for change 2.

## Outside sources

- **Adopted:** "judge the experience from the user's point of view, not the implementer's", from
  [popey's ux-audit skill](https://tessl.io/registry/skills/github/popey/claude-code-skills/ux-audit).
  This is the framing of the planner's review.
- **Adopted:** "require reproducible steps" for each finding, from
  [Daniela Napoli's heuristic evaluation post](https://hellodaniela.substack.com/p/ai-heuristic-evaluation-building).
- **Not adopted:** a full Nielsen 10-heuristic audit per card. It is long, and three questions
  cover what was missed.
