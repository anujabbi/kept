## Who should have caught them

No step in the pipeline is asked to judge usability, so every agent did its job and these still
got through:

- **Reviewer:** reads code only.
- **Dev and QA:** prove the acceptance list. Every layout check we have (Daybreak's "fit and
  finish" list, the goldens, the font-scale checks) asks "is anything clipped or overlapping?",
  never "does this get enough room?".
- **Planner:** turns the owner's design into a spec.

Each problem had an owner, and in my opinion each was seen and let go:

| Problem | Where it was decided | Who saw it | What happened |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unit hidden for preset weekly goals | **Planner's** #206 plan copied the habit screen's `showsUnits` rule | Dev flagged it in the PR | **Reviewer** approved it as "loose wording" in the acceptance list. The onboarding sheet's missing target control traces back to #205's own design. |
| Not enough room on Home | **Dev** on #174: a `DECISIONS.md` line says the add row sits half below the fold with two habits and needs a scroll with three | Reviewer, and QA on #174 | Both recorded it as fact. The 22 Nov EM report saw that "the weekly section is below the fold in every one" of the Home goldens and treated it as noise. |

The step best placed to catch these is **QA**. It is the only one that looks at the running app
end to end, and its #207 comment had the screenshots of Home. When QA does notice usability, it
writes a note and moves on: 10 notes in the window, and 6 never became a card (#123 chip, #128,
#132, #134, #168, #207). That is the repeated cause. The fix belongs to me as EM: no skill asked
anyone to take that look.

## Changes (PR for #215, needs the owner)

1. **`qa` step 6a: look at the end screen as a first-time user.** On the screen the flow ends on,
   at 412 × 915dp and font 1.0, QA asks three questions. Does what this card added show on the
   first screen? Can the user change everything the screen shows about it? Would a new user know
   what to do next? Each "no" becomes its own Backlog card with the `usability` label, steps and
   a screenshot. It never blocks the card under test. **Incidents:** the 6 lost notes, and today's
   two problems.
2. **`dev` step 7: a trade-off the user can see goes under "For the owner" in the PR body.** That
   covers a control hidden or dropped, or something pushed below the fold. QA files each one as a
   `usability` card. **Incidents:** #174 (add row below the fold) and #206 (unit chips hidden).
3. **`em-stats.sh`: a `?` inside a link, code or a quote no longer counts as a question.** The
   count for this window goes from 4 to 0.

Nothing removed this pass. The reviewer stays code-only (the owner's decision of 3 Oct). The
`usability` label was created for change 1.

## Outside sources

- **Adopted:** "judge the experience from the user's point of view, not the implementer's", from
  [popey's ux-audit skill](https://tessl.io/registry/skills/github/popey/claude-code-skills/ux-audit),
  which became the framing of step 6a.
- **Adopted:** "require reproducible steps" for each finding, and keep severity apart from finding
  it, from [Daniela Napoli's heuristic evaluation post](https://hellodaniela.substack.com/p/ai-heuristic-evaluation-building).
- **Not adopted:** a full Nielsen 10-heuristic audit per card. It adds a long step to every card,
  and three questions cover what was missed.
